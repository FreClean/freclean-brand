# Photography Guidelines

## What FreClean photography should communicate

Professional, clean, warm — real cleaning teams doing real work, real products in real (or realistic) settings, and Haiti's own light and context, not a generic stock-photo aesthetic.

## Current photography

| Image type | Status |
|---|---|
| Product lifestyle shots (bottle on counter, with lime/citrus styling) | Available — in use on `freclean-website` and `freclean-products` |
| Team-at-work shots (office, bedroom turnover) | Available — in use on `freclean-website` Services page |
| Storefront / distribution shots | Available — in use on `freclean-entrepreneurship` context |
| Founder / leadership photography | Not yet available |
| Real customer testimonial photography | Not yet available |

## Style guide

- **Lighting:** bright, natural light — avoid heavy artificial flash or overly staged studio lighting.
- **Color grading:** true-to-life color, consistent with the brand palette's blues and greens where props/styling allow (e.g. blue cleaning liquid, green accents), but never color-corrected in a way that misrepresents the actual product color.
- **People:** real FreClean team members and, with consent, real customers — never stock photography of unrelated people presented as FreClean staff or customers.
- **Composition:** leave clear space for text overlay when a photo is intended for a hero banner (see `freclean-website`'s hero layout for the current standard).

## Product photography specifics

- Show the label clearly and legibly.
- Include true-to-size context (a hand, a countertop) so viewers can gauge bottle size.
- Never digitally alter a product photo to show a label claim (certification, ingredient, size) that isn't accurate — this is the same realism rule applied to imagery, not just text.

## What not to do

- Don't use AI-generated or stock imagery to depict a specific, named FreClean product, team member, or facility as if it were a real photograph, once real photography exists for that subject.
- Don't crop or edit a photo in a way that misrepresents scale, quantity, or condition.
